Most people picture Ibiza in August: full marinas, €18 cocktails, boats rafted three deep in every bay. October is a different island. The charter fleet has thinned out, the anchorages are half empty, and you can still swim without thinking twice about it.

We sail Ibiza and Formentera from  **10 to 17 October 2026**  aboard a  *Fountaine Pajot Lucia 40* , as part of a flotilla of 5 to 8 yachts. Here is the route, the cost, the weather, and who the week suits.

## 01. Why October

Air temperatures average around  **24°C in the day**  and drop to about  **15°C at night** . The sea sits near  **22 to 23°C** , warm enough to swim off the back of the boat every day without a wetsuit.

October is also the wettest month in Ibiza, around 50mm of rain across nine or ten days. In practice that usually means a short heavy shower rather than a grey week. We check the forecast every morning and move the route around it, which is one advantage of not booking a fixed itinerary.

                &ldquo;In August you queue for a mooring buoy in Cala Saona. In October you pick your spot.&rdquo;

## 02. What a flotilla actually is

Five to eight yachts sail the same route on the same dates. Each yacht has its own skipper and its own crew of up to eight people. You get a small group on your boat, and  **40 to 50 people across the fleet**  who you see at anchor, at dinner, and on the beach.

That solves the problem most solo travellers have with sailing holidays. You are not stuck with seven strangers for seven days with nowhere else to go. If your boat is quiet one evening, another boat is having dinner ashore.

We are joining the  **Elmár Sailing Flotilla**  for their Ibiza route. They have been running this format for years, and the social side of it is why people come back.

## 03. The boat: Fountaine Pajot Lucia 40

A  **Fountaine Pajot Lucia 40** , built in 2019. Four double cabins, two bathrooms, eight guests maximum.

We run a catamaran rather than a monohull because it does not heel. If you have never sailed, that removes the most common source of discomfort, which is the boat leaning over at 20 degrees while you try to make coffee. You can sit in the cockpit with a drink while we are under sail and nothing slides off the table.

The saloon and cockpit open into each other, so eight people can eat together without anyone perched on a step. The foredeck trampolines are where most of the afternoon gets spent.

## 04. The route

We start and finish in  **Ibiza Town (Eivissa)** . The route changes with the wind, but this is the shape of it:

                   ![Ibiza Town Dalt Vila](../images/ibiza-town.png)

                    Starting Point

### Ibiza Town (Dalt Vila)

The old fortified quarter of Dalt Vila above the port is worth an evening on its own. If you can, fly in a day early and see it before you get on the boat.

                   ![Formentera pale turquoise water](../images/formentera.png)

                    Island Anchorage

### Formentera

Shallow, pale water over Posidonia seagrass beds, as clear as the photographs suggest. We spend a slow day there swimming, snorkelling and walking the beach.

                   ![Es Vedrà limestone rock](../images/es-vedra.png)

                    Iconic Landmark

### Es Vedrà

The mystical limestone rock off the southwest coast. We anchor nearby and take the paddleboards out. It is the photograph everyone comes home with.

                   ![Cala Saona red cliffs](../images/cala-saona.png)

                    Quiet Cove

### Cala Saona

Red cliffs, a beach club if you want one, and a pine forest hike to a viewpoint if you don't. In October, calm anchor spots are easy to claim.

                   ![Sant Antoni sunset](../images/san-antonio.png)

                    Sunset Landmark

### Sant Antoni de Portmany

We time our arrival at Sant Antoni for sunset. Café del Mar is on the waterfront there, and the sunset is a world-renowned local institution.

                   ![Espalmador nature reserve dunes](../images/espalmador.png)

                    Nature Reserve

### Espalmador

A wild bay in a protected reserve with rolling dunes and nothing built on it. Pure sand spit isolation where the drone comes out.

## 05. A day on board

Wake up at anchor, swim before breakfast, coffee in the cockpit. We leave for the next bay mid-morning, sail three to five hours, and anchor by mid-afternoon. Then swimming, paddleboarding and a nap. Dinner on board or ashore, depending on where we are.

#### Hands-on sailing or pure leisure?

Nobody has to do anything. If you want to helm, trim sails and learn to read the wind, I will teach you and you will be doing real jobs by day three. If you want to lie on the trampoline for seven days with a book, that is a perfectly good way to spend the week.

## 06. What it costs

The berth is  **€890 per person** . That includes the yacht charter and insurance, a professional skipper, the dinghy and outboard, bed linen and towels, final cleaning, sailing instruction, and help planning your travel before the trip.

On top of that there is an operating kitty of about  **€200** , shared across the crew, which covers fuel, marina and mooring fees, and provisions for breakfasts and lunches on board.

Flights, transfers and dinners in restaurants are not included. Flights to Ibiza in October cost much less than in summer, and booking ahead usually gets you a return fare under €150 from most of Europe.

## 07. Do you need sailing experience?

**No.**  Most people on these trips have never sailed. I hold international ISS skipper credentials and I am trained on both monohulls and catamarans. I ran our Sicily 2026 flotilla on a Lagoon 38 without incident, and we have moved up to the Lucia 40 for the extra space.

You do need to swim, and to be mobile enough to climb on and off a boat, up ladders, and in and out of a dinghy several times a day.

## 08. Who this isn't for

We believe in complete transparency before you book:

- If you want a private cabin and total silence, a flotilla is the wrong format.
- If you are travelling with small children, this particular trip is not built for it.
- If your idea of Ibiza is the West End clubs at 3am, a hotel will serve you better, because we are anchored in empty bays most nights.

## 09. What to bring

Bring a  **soft bag rather than a hard suitcase** , because there is nowhere on a boat to store a rigid case.

                  &check; Deck shoes or bare feet (no black soles)

                  &check; Two swimsuits (so one is always dry)

                  &check; A light jacket or windbreaker for evenings

                  &check; Reef-safe sunscreen & polarized sunglasses

                  &check; A personal power bank (sockets on board are shared)
